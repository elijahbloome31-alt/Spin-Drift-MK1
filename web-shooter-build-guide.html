SPIN-DRIFT MK I — STEP-BY-STEP BUILD

How to fabricate, assemble, wire, and safely test the nylon-rope web shooter.

Skill: beginner–intermediate Time: 2 weekends (~15–20 h) Tools: 3D printer / drill press, soldering iron Cost: ≈$116 base build

0Read first — what this is (and isn't)

The Spin-Drift Mk I is a spring-powered, single-dart launcher that trails a braided nylon line, with a small motor that reels the line back in. It is a hobby/engineering and cosplay device.

  • It fires one blunt dart on a line. It does not shoot a web, a rope of webbing, or a net.
  • It is not rated for climbing, swinging, pulling, or holding any weight. Even though the line is rated to ~200 lb, the anchor, spool, and wrist mount are not load-bearing. Never tie off or rappel on it.
  • Launch energy is ~2–5 J (spring) — comparable to a low-power airsoft pistol. The tip is blunt, but it can still bruise or hurt an eye. Treat it like a slingshot.
  • Check your local laws before building or carrying it. Some places regulate spring/CO₂ projectors and grappling-type launchers.
Golden rules while building and testing: never point it at people or animals · always wear eye protection · test into a soft backstop · never load a dart unless you intend to fire safely · store it unloaded with the safety engaged.

1Tools & materials

Tools

  • ☐ FDM 3D printer (PETG) — or a drill press + hacksaw + files for aluminum
  • ☐ Soldering iron + solder + heat-shrink tubing
  • ☐ Digital calipers
  • ☐ Drill + bit set (1.5–6 mm) + M3 tap
  • ☐ Hex keys / screwdrivers, needle-nose pliers, snips
  • ☐ Dremel or files for deburring
  • ☐ Hacksaw (to cut barrel and dart rod)
  • ☐ Epoxy (5-min) + threadlocker (blue)
  • ☐ Sandpaper (180–400 grit)

Materials (matches BOM in the design doc)

  • ☐ PETG filament ≈150 g, or 6061 aluminum for the shell
  • ☐ 12 mm ID × 16 mm OD aluminum tube, 150 mm (barrel)
  • ☐ Airsoft "M120" mainspring (Ø≈11 mm × 150 mm)
  • ☐ Delrin/acetal rod Ø12 mm (piston) + nitrile o-ring Ø12 mm
  • ☐ Steel rod Ø6 mm × 65 mm (dart) + Ø6×2 mm neodymium magnet
  • ☐ 2 mm braided nylon accessory cord, ~10 m (see capacity note, §4)
  • ☐ 2 × 688-ZZ ball bearings
  • ☐ N20 geared DC motor, ~300 rpm @ 6 V + HF0612 one-way bearing
  • ☐ 2S LiPo 7.4 V 350 mAh + 2S USB balance charger board
  • ☐ Micro push-button or slide switch + 1 A polyfuse
  • ☐ 50 mm padded strap + Velcro + 3 mm foam
  • ☐ M3 hardware (screws, heat-set inserts, washers), 2 mm spring steel for sear
Print settings for structural parts: PETG, 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layer height, 4 walls, 40–60 % infill, supports on overhangs, and heat-set M3 inserts in all screw bosses. Print the two housing shells flat with the seam along the top rail.

2Fabricate the parts

Make all components before you start assembling. Work from the key dimensions table below — these are the numbers everything else keys off of.

2.1 Housing shells

Print or machine the two shell halves

Left shell carries the spool axle and battery; right shell carries the motor, sear, and trigger. Include a 16 mm half-round groove along the top edge of each half so the barrel clamps between them, and a Ø20 mm bore at the front for the muzzle. Add a slot for the trigger and a channel for the safety slide. If machining aluminum, drill and tap M3 holes instead of using heat-set inserts.

2.2 Barrel

Cut and prepare the launch tube

Cut the 12/16 mm tube to 150 mm. Deburr both ends and lightly chamfer the breech (rear) end so the piston o-ring slides in without nicking. Using a file, cut a sear slot — a 6 mm × 20 mm opening on the top of the barrel, starting ~10 mm from the breech — the sear hook reaches through this slot to catch the piston. Smooth all slot edges.

2.3 Piston

Turn the piston from Delrin

Turn Ø12 mm Delrin to Ø11.8 mm over a 45 mm length. Cut an o-ring groove 3 mm from the front face (Ø10.8 mm × 2 mm wide) and fit the Ø12 mm o-ring. At the rear, cut a square catch shoulder: reduce the last 8 mm of the piston to Ø9 mm so a vertical 4 mm face remains for the sear to grab. Drill a Ø3 mm × 15 mm pilot hole in the rear face if you add a spring guide rod.

2.4 Dart

Make the grappling dart

Cut 60–65 mm of Ø6 mm steel rod. Round the front end into a smooth dome (grind + sand, no sharp point). Drill a Ø2 mm cross-hole 5 mm from the rear for the line, and a Ø6 × 2 mm recess at the tip for the neodymium magnet — epoxy the magnet in so the dart sticks to steel targets for retrieval. Target weight 5–9 g.

2.5 Sear & trigger

Cut and bend the sear hook and trigger bar

From 2 mm spring steel, cut a hook ~6 mm wide with a short 90° lip that drops through the barrel's sear slot and catches the piston shoulder. Drill a Ø3 mm pivot hole. From 2 mm aluminum, cut a trigger bar that runs from the front trigger lever back to the sear, with a Ø3 mm pivot at each end. Cut the safety slide from 3 mm plastic or steel: a small block that slides under the trigger to lock it.

3Frame & wrist mount

3.1 Fit the strap

Rivet or screw the 50 mm strap to the underside of the housing through two M3 bosses. Glue 3 mm foam to the inside of the strap so the housing sits comfortably over your forearm, barrel pointing along the back of your hand. Leave 150 mm of strap + Velcro for adjustment.

3.2 Join the shells

Dry-fit the two shells around the barrel, spool axle, and motor boss. When everything lines up, thread M3 screws into the heat-set inserts with a drop of blue threadlocker. Do not glue the shells — you need to open them for maintenance.

3.3 Set the barrel clamp

Clamp the barrel in its top-rail groove so the sear slot points up and the breech sits ~5 mm behind the sear pivot. The barrel should be firm but removable.

4Line system (spool + line)

4.1 Press in the bearings

Press one 688-ZZ bearing into the left hub of the spool and fit it onto the fixed stub axle on the left shell. The right hub gets the Ø12 mm recess for the one-way bearing (see §6). The spool must spin freely with very little friction.

4.2 Add light drag (anti-bird's-nest)

Place a thin felt washer or a wavy spring washer between the spool and the left shell, adjusted by a screw through the axle. This light drag keeps the spool from over-spinning when the dart pays the line out fast.

4.3 Wind the line

Knot the line to the spool core through a Ø2 mm hole (clove hitch + a drop of superglue). Wind it on under firm, even tension by turning the spool. Tie the free end to the dart last (§8).

Line capacity is real physics — pick your trade-off: the Ø40 × 25 mm spool holds roughly 8–10 m of 2 mm (200 lb) cord, ~15–18 m of 1.5 mm (120 lb), or ~25–30 m of 1 mm (60 lb). For prop use and indoor testing, 1–1.5 mm reaches farther; for the strongest line, accept the shorter 2 mm range. (The design doc's "25 m" assumes 1–1.5 mm line.)

5Launch train (barrel + spring + piston + sear)

5.1 Load the spring and piston

Slide the mainspring into the barrel from the breech, then the piston on top of it. Compress the piston with a push rod until its rear shoulder passes the sear slot. Do not use your thumb over the muzzle — the barrel is open and the piston is under spring load.

5.2 Install the sear

Mount the sear on its pivot pin so the lip drops through the barrel slot. Fit a small return spring (or use a rubber band) that pushes the sear into the piston shoulder. Check that the sear catches the piston's 4 mm face squarely — full face contact, not just the corner.

5.3 Link the trigger

Connect the trigger bar between the trigger lever and the sear tail. Set the geometry so ~4 mm of trigger travel rotates the sear clear of the piston. Adjust pivot holes with a round file until the release is clean and the trigger returns on its own.

Never assemble or test the launch train pointing at yourself or anyone else. Even unloaded, the piston can pinch fingers if it slips. Keep the safety slide engaged whenever the launcher is cocked.

6Rewind drive (motor + one-way clutch)

6.1 Mount the motor

Bolt the N20 motor into its boss on the right shell so its output shaft is coaxial with the spool axle (see Fig. C). If your motor is offset, add a 3:1 gear or friction roller to drive the spool rim — coaxial is simpler and recommended.

6.2 Set the one-way bearing

Press the HF0612 one-way bearing onto the motor shaft, then press the spool's right hub onto the bearing's outer race. Check the direction by hand: with the motor unpowered, the spool must spin freely in the payout direction, and it must lock and turn when you spin the motor shaft in the reel direction. If it's backwards, flip the bearing.

6.3 Verify the freewheel

Pull 1 m of line off the spool by hand. It should pay out with only the light drag you set in §4.2 and must not back-drive the motor. Then power the motor briefly and confirm it reels the line in smoothly.

7Electronics (battery + switch + charging)

7.1 Wire it up

Solder in this order: battery + → 1 A polyfuse → switch → motor +; motor − → battery −. Solder the 2S balance charger board to the battery leads (B+/B−) and mount its USB-C port where you can reach it. Insulate every joint with heat-shrink. A momentary push button (hold to reel) is the safest switch choice.

7.2 Secure and test

Strap the LiPo into the left shell with the charger board accessible. Before closing the shell, test the reel with the barrel empty and the line slack. If the motor runs hot or slow, check for binding in the spool or a backwards one-way bearing.

8Line routing & the dart

8.1 Fit the ceramic guide

Mount the ceramic eyelet at the muzzle mouth so the line exits straight along the barrel axis with no sharp edges to saw through the cord. A second guide at the spool keeps the line feeding off evenly.

8.2 Tie the dart on

Pass the line through the dart's 2 mm hole and tie a figure-eight follow-through (or improved clinch), then lock the knot with a drop of superglue. Keep the knot small so it never jams the barrel. Pull-test to ~10 kg before the first live shot.

8.3 Check the snag path

Slowly hand-pull the line from the spool through both guides. It must run freely with no rubbing on screw heads, shell edges, or the sear. Relieve any edge that touches the line.

9Calibration & tuning

  • Sear engagement: start with 2 mm of overlap on the piston shoulder, then increase until the cocked piston holds reliably but still releases with a light trigger pull (~1–2 kg).
  • Trigger weight & travel: adjust the trigger bar length/pivot so release happens in the first half of the trigger's travel.
  • Line drag: tighten the spool drag just enough to prevent over-spin; too much drag shortens the throw.
  • Reel tension: confirm the motor can pull the dart back across a smooth floor without stalling; if it stalls, add a 3:1 gear or use a lower-geared (higher-torque) N20.
Expected performance (be honest with yourself): a dart at ~5–9 g with ~3–5 J leaves the muzzle around 25–35 m/s and travels roughly 20–30 m on an arc. The line follows the dart — it does not support weight and is not a swing line.

10Safe test protocol

  1. Unloaded function checks first. Cock and dry-fire (no dart, no line tension) to verify the sear, trigger, and safety all work 10× in a row.
  2. Indoor low-power shot. Load 3 m of line. Fire into a cardboard box stuffed with foam/rags at 1 m range. Check the line pays out cleanly and the reel retrieves it.
  3. Medium shot. 8 m of line into the same backstop at 3 m. Check for tangles and confirm the dart lands tip-first (blunt) without excessive tumbling.
  4. Full-range shot. Outdoors, with a clear 40 m lane, no people or animals, fire into a soft backstop (foam board, hay bale). Confirm range and retrieval.
  5. After every session: unload, engage safety, wipe the barrel, and check the line for fraying near the dart knot and muzzle guide. Replace the line if it shows wear.
Stop if anything feels wrong. If the dart jams, if the line snaps, or if the sear won't hold, do not "try harder" — take the device apart and inspect. A snapped line under spring load can whip back toward you; always wear eye protection and gloves for the first full-power shots.

12Maintenance, storage & optional upgrades

Maintenance

  • Wipe the barrel bore with a dry cloth after sessions; a drop of silicone oil on the piston o-ring keeps the seal smooth (don't oil the sear — it must stay dry).
  • Inspect the line before every session; retire it at the first sign of fraying or glazing.
  • Check M3 screws for loosening; re-apply threadlocker as needed.

Storage

  • Store uncocked, safety engaged, dart removed.
  • Store the LiPo at ~3.8 V/cell for long idle periods; never leave it charging unattended.

Optional upgrades (advanced builders only)

  • Stronger spring (M130–M140): more range, but re-tune the sear and re-test for safety.
  • CO₂ boost module: the design doc lists this as optional. It raises energy to ~8 J — build it only if you are experienced with pneumatic/CO₂ devices, and re-do the entire test protocol. For most users the spring build is the right stopping point.
  • Quick-change spool: a second pre-wound spool for instant line swaps.
